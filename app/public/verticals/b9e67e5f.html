Enable efficient prescriber and pharmacist collaboration

Get patients the medications they need sooner by integrating accurate, consistently formatted prescription data into existing software. Leverage the nation’s largest and most active e-prescribing network, connecting virtually all electronic health records (EHRs), health systems, pharmacies, health information exchanges and long-term and post-acute care facilities.

In-workflow tools help pharmacists and prescribers collaborate efficiently using NCPDP-defined transactions, including NewRx, RxFill, RxChange, RxTransfer, RxRenewal, NewRxRequest and CancelRx. Tap into industry-leading patient matching, automation that intelligently structures prescription data, and other innovations built to help improve medication adherence and patient safety.

How it works for EHR vendors

A prescriber writes a prescription and sends it from within your E-Prescribing module.

Formatted with standards built for clarity and safety, the prescription is routed to the patient’s preferred pharmacy.

The prescriber handles any requests to change or clarify the prescription within your EHR.

RxChange lets prescribers review and quickly respond to questions and recommendations from the pharmacy.

The patient picks up the prescription, and the prescriber is notified.

The RxFill transaction provides certainty that the patient has picked up their medication.

The provider receives renewal or new prescription requests.

When a pharmacy sends a renewal or new prescription request on behalf of the patient, it’s easy to approve in the EHR.

When the medication is no longer needed, the prescriber can cancel it electronically.

CancelRx tells the pharmacy to discontinue prescriptions or refills that the patient no longer needs.

Feature in focus

Protect prescriptions during downtime

Our message queue service holds e-prescribing messages in a queue if there is an outage or service interruption that prevents them from being processed by the receiver.

Prevent extra administrative work

With our message queue service, planned or unplanned disruptions don’t trigger manual work. The service holds prescriptions, then sends them in order once the processer’s system is able to receive them.

No provider, pharmacist or patient disruption

Providers don’t need to send faxes or paper prescriptions, pharmacies don’t need to manually enter lost information, and patients may never need to know about the outage at all.
